# RITES — Mortuary, Ceremony & Priority Handoff Continuum

## Purpose

RITES must coordinate what happens when a person becomes absent without reducing the person to a funeral case. Mortuary care, identification, ceremony, attendance, publication, possessions, confidential material, confessions, advice, memories and unfinished intentions are all continuity objects that may require different handoffs.

## 1. Mortuary Continuum

The platform models authorized handoffs among:

- identifier / confirming authority
- family or authorized representative
- hospital or clinical authority
- morgue / mortuary
- mortician / funeral director
- cemetery / cremation / disposition provider
- transport provider
- religious officiant
- ceremony team
- printer / stationery provider
- florist / memorial provider
- government or legal authority

Each handoff records authority, timing, location, responsibility, required evidence, status, cost and completion evidence.

RITES must never itself certify death, identify a body, determine cause of death, or authorize disposition. Those are authoritative workflows belonging to the appropriate medical, legal, governmental, religious or other competent authority. RITES coordinates verified outcomes and routes the next authorized action.

## 2. Identification and verification

Support a chain of evidence for identity and death status:

`reported event -> competent confirmation -> identity evidence -> authoritative record -> RITES transition`

Conflicting or incomplete information enters an `inconclusive` state and cannot silently activate irreversible workflows.

## 3. Attendance Priority Graph

Attendance is not a flat guest list. A person may be:

- required
- explicitly wished for
- committed
- priority
- invited
- optional
- remote
- unable to attend
- deceased / unavailable
- restricted

A Continuum event can calculate an attendance priority list from the person's recorded wishes, relationship context, ceremony role, geographic constraints and authorized family decisions.

The system must distinguish **wish** from **obligation**. A stated wish should be surfaced prominently and pursued through appropriate coordination, but RITES cannot guarantee that another human will attend or that a provider can fulfill an impossible request.

For people abroad or otherwise remote, the system can offer authorized alternatives such as remote participation, recorded contribution, livestream, message delivery or delegated ceremony roles.

## 4. Ceremony Composer

A ceremony is a structured Continuum rather than a generic event.

It may contain:

- faith / cultural rite
- officiant
- speakers
- eulogy
- readings
- music
- band / musicians
- procession
- transport
- coffin / casket preference where lawful and available
- clothing
- flowers
- photographs
- video
- livestream
- printed materials
- souvenirs
- food / gathering
- venue
- cemetery / mausoleum / cremation destination
- burial instructions
- people assigned to specific duties
- publication permissions
- privacy restrictions

Each item becomes a handoff or task with an owner and deadline.

## 5. News and Publication Control

RITES should support a **Publication Gate** before any public announcement or social post.

For each media item or announcement:

- who may publish
- who must approve
- intended audience
- release time
- wording / framing
- image selection
- caption
- RIP / memorial treatment
- platforms
- people to notify first
- people who must not learn through public media
- private-only material
- prohibited material
- embargo

The system can recommend an order of notification, but it must not autonomously disclose a death or sensitive family information without explicit authority and appropriate verification.

## 6. Media Curator

Photos, video and audio are Continuum objects with relationship and publication permissions.

A person may specify:

- post publicly
- family only
- specific person(s)
- ceremony only
- private archive
- timed release
- never publish
- delete after event
- preserve indefinitely

RITES can curate candidate media based on explicit rules, but final publication of sensitive material requires the configured authority gate.

## 7. Confidential Continuum

Support sealed material such as:

- private confession
- personal advice
- family history
- sensitive memories
- unresolved relationships
- private letters
- secrets intended for a specific recipient
- material explicitly marked to remain sealed

Every confidential object has a release policy:

`recipient + condition + trigger + authority + release window + fallback`

A secret is not automatically disclosed merely because a person has died. Death is a trigger only when the creator explicitly made it one.

## 8. Family discovery and legacy offspring

RITES may record an undisclosed or unverified relationship claim, including a possible child or other family relationship, without treating the claim as established fact.

States:

`asserted -> evidence required -> verified -> disputed -> rejected`

Potential inheritance or beneficiary consequences must route to the appropriate legal process. RITES must never label someone as an heir, child or beneficiary solely because a resemblance, message or unverified claim suggests it.

Where legally and ethically appropriate, authorized genealogy/DNA services may be integrated as evidence sources. DNA data remains highly sensitive and requires explicit consent, strict access control and provenance.

## 9. What travels with the deceased

Create a **Disposition Inventory** for things intended to accompany the person, remain with the family, enter an archive, or be transferred elsewhere.

Categories can include:

- clothing
- religious objects
- letters
- photographs
- symbolic objects
- permitted personal possessions
- keepsakes
- documents where lawful
- memorial objects

Each item has a disposition rule and confirmation requirement. RITES does not determine what may legally be buried, cremated or transported; local law, cemetery rules, religious requirements and provider constraints remain authoritative.

## 10. Confessions, advice and experience

RITES can curate a private **Wisdom Continuum** containing:

- advice
- life lessons
- professional experience
- family knowledge
- instructions
- stories
- encouragement
- recorded memories
- future guidance

Release can be immediate, delayed, event-triggered or permanently sealed.

The AI may organize, summarize and route authorized material, but must preserve provenance and must not fabricate a person's words or imply that generated text was actually spoken by them.

## 11. Continuous intelligence

The Continuum Intelligence Engine continuously checks:

- missing identity verification
- missing mortuary handoff
- unassigned ceremony duties
- priority attendees not contacted
- unresolved travel/remote attendance
- missing publication approval
- media conflicts
- unfulfilled wishes
- provider deadlines
- disposition conflicts
- unresolved legal flags
- sealed material with no valid release policy
- duplicate or contradictory instructions
- tasks without accountable owners

The loop is:

`detect -> curate -> explain -> recommend -> authorize -> handoff -> verify -> record -> continue`

## 12. Guardrails

RITES must separate:

- wish vs authority
- claim vs fact
- memory vs evidence
- recommendation vs action
- private vs public
- family preference vs legal requirement
- AI inference vs verified information

High-impact actions require an appropriate human or institutional authority. Examples include death registration, body identification, public announcement, disposition authorization, legal inheritance decisions, release of sensitive records, account closure and irreversible deletion.

## 13. Long-run Continuum

After the ceremony, the record does not terminate.

It can continue into:

- anniversary reminders
- grave visits
- maintenance
- flowers
- memorial events
- family gatherings
- timed messages
- promises and commitments
- provisions
- genealogy
- archives
- digital estate outcomes
- future family milestones
- fulfilled wishes

The objective is not to simulate the deceased as an autonomous person. It is to preserve and responsibly activate the person's authorized intentions, memories and commitments.

## Canonical principle

**RITES does not merely record what happened. It coordinates what was intended, what must be handed off, what has been fulfilled, what remains unresolved, and what continues.**
