# RITES Product Surface V1

RITES is rendered as a contextual Continuum, not a fixed generic dashboard. A user's available surfaces are composed from their authorized roles, relationships, active scenarios and responsibilities.

## Primary surfaces

### 1. Continuum Home
The living starting point. Shows what matters now, active people, current responsibilities, upcoming events, unresolved handoffs, recent changes and recommended next actions.

### 2. People
A relationship-centered graph for family, friends, caregivers, professionals, institutions and communities. Each relationship has context, provenance and authority rather than being merely a contact record.

### 3. Life Archive
A chronological and thematic record of life: diaries, handwriting, signatures, photographs, letters, certificates, education, work, achievements, creations, travel, stories, audio/video, memorabilia and physical objects.

### 4. Wishes & Intentions
Wants, needs, musts, preferences, vows, promises, oaths, instructions, provisions and conditional/timed intentions. Distinguish preference, commitment, legal instrument and authoritative instruction.

### 5. Continuity Planner
Scenario planning for self, family, caregiver, executor and professional roles. Supports incapacity, serious illness, death, death abroad, family transition, ceremony, estate settlement and long-run continuation.

### 6. Transition Desk
A controlled workspace for registration, identification evidence, mortuary, transport, ceremony, disposition, religious rites, legal follow-up, autopsy/exhumation workflows and authorized donation processes.

### 7. Ceremony Composer
Coordinates faith/culture, officiant, readings, eulogy, music, band, procession, transport, clothing, flowers, photographs, video, printing, livestream, souvenirs and attendance.

### 8. Grave & Place
Find, register, map, visit, maintain and document graves and memorial places. Supports maintenance requests, flowers, tombstones, inscriptions, anniversaries, family plots and authorized exhumation records.

### 9. Digital Estate
Inventory digital identities and assets. Define preserve, memorialize, transfer, close, archive, scrub, publish or delete intentions. Provider-specific actions are executed through adapters and verified handoffs.

### 10. Heritage
Physical and digital preservation, including memorabilia, objects, clothing, medals, artwork, books, manuscripts, family recipes, recordings, framed photographs and heirlooms.

### 11. Lineage
Genealogy, family history, community history and DNA adapter integrations. Genetic data remains highly sensitive and is never treated as ordinary genealogy data.

### 12. Provision
Education, housing, healthcare, ceremony, family obligations and other provisions. Links intentions to resources, conditions, beneficiaries and fulfillment evidence.

### 13. Marketplace
Need -> request -> provider -> offer -> agreement -> booking -> payment/provision -> fulfillment -> evidence -> settlement.

### 14. Intelligence
Natural-language entry, classification, relationship discovery, gap detection, scenario simulation, prioritization, provider matching, reporting and workflow coordination.

### 15. Reports
Role-specific reports for individuals/families, executors, providers, religious institutions, cemeteries, legal professionals, healthcare/public-health authorities, government and authorized researchers.

## Contextual modes

A user can simultaneously occupy multiple modes. Examples:

- Self + daughter + caregiver
- Self + executor + business owner
- Parent + beneficiary + patient
- Pastor + family member + ceremony coordinator
- Government employee + citizen + authorized public-health operator
- Funeral director + family member
- Cemetery operator + memorial curator

RITES composes the view from these contexts instead of forcing the user into one account type.

## What Matters Now

The default intelligence surface ranks:

1. urgent safety/care items
2. time-bound authorized obligations
3. active handoffs blocked by missing information
4. upcoming ceremonies/events
5. expiring permissions or provisions
6. unresolved estate/digital/heritage tasks
7. long-run commitments and anniversaries
8. opportunities to preserve meaningful history

Recommendations explain why they appeared and never silently execute consequential actions.

## Continuum state machine

`draft -> active -> pending -> authorized -> executing -> verified -> fulfilled -> continuing -> archived`

Exceptional states include `disputed`, `blocked`, `expired`, `revoked`, `sealed` and `superseded`.

## Experience principle

The public website tells the story. The application tells the person what matters now. The underlying graph remembers why.
